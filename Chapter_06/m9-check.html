Module 6.9 · Assessment

Independent check

Six concept questions, two scenarios, one practical on new data, and one spoken explanation. Answer under the stated assumptions. The concept questions give instant feedback so you can self-check; the scenarios, practical and oral are marked by your instructor from the chapter’s Instructor Appendix.

Pass rule (from the blueprint)

  • Suggested pass: 80 of 100 — and no critical misconception.
  • Critical misconceptions that block progress on their own: treating CRS assignment as reprojection; treating degrees as metres; guessing a CRS and carrying on; claiming on-screen alignment justifies analysis; reporting a flat Web Mercator figure as ground distance without stating the error.

6.9.1Concept questions (30 points, 5 each)

Q1 (6.1). A projected CRS is described as “conformal”. Which statement is necessarily true?
Q2 (6.2). A Shapefile arrives without its .prj. The survey office’s documentation confirms it exports in EPSG:32643. You need it in EPSG:4326. Which sequence is right?
Q3 (6.3). Which four things must be inspected in a datum-transformation record before accepting it?
Q4 (6.4). Two layers, one in EPSG:4326 and one in EPSG:32643, draw exactly on top of each other. What does that alignment prove? (a) both converted to one CRS; (b) both labels consistent with each other at screen resolution; (c) ready for a 500 m proximity analysis; (d) neither layer’s stored values changed by being added.
Q5 (6.5). From an EPSG:4326 layer a developer computes √(Δlon² + Δlat²) = 0.010 and reports “about 1.1 km apart”. What is wrong, and how do you get metres without changing the stored data?
Q6 (6.6). Team 1: a 1.2 km drainage line inside one ward, EPSG:32643, to the nearest metre. Team 2: straight-line distance from a national centre to 3,000 depots across India, EPSG:4326, to the nearest km. Choose for each.

6.9.2Scenario questions (20 points, 10 each) — write your answers; instructor-marked

Scenario 1 (6.2–6.3)

Three files arrive for the same town.

  • File K: lat/lon pairs, header “coordinates: WGS84”, provenance “from the 2004 survey adjustment (Kalianpur 1975)”.
  • File L: E/N pairs near (500,000, 2,544,000), no CRS statement, provenance “exported from the request tracker; the tracker stores UTM 43N”.
  • File M: lat/lon pairs, header “EPSG:4326”, provenance “captured last month by a phone app in WGS 84”.

For each file say whether it is unknown CRS, wrongly assigned CRS, or known CRS needing conversion, and give the different correct response to each: what you do, what you refuse to do, what you write in the log.

Scenario 2 (6.4–6.6)

A manager sees the wards and requests aligned in a web map (Web Mercator basemap) and asks you to “just buffer the road by 500 m in the web map and count requests inside — it’s all lined up”. In no more than eight sentences: what the alignment does and does not establish; what a 500 m buffer computed flat in Web Mercator at 23° N would really cover on the ground (give the approximate error from 6.5); and two acceptable ways to get a correct 500 m buffer. Then explain separately why latitude/longitude request data would still support a correct geodesic answer.

Required work

  1. Inspect all three files and classify each as known CRS, unknown CRS or wrongly labelled, with evidence. Say what you will and will not do with legacy_z.csv.
  2. Choose an analysis CRS with the 6.6 worksheet (justify the zone — the 6.6 zone finder helps).
  3. Make an analysis-ready copy of requests_z in that CRS by transformation, and build the zone polygon from zone_z.csv. Report R1–R4’s projected coordinates to 0.001 m.
  4. Measure R1–R2 and R3–R4 (flat in the analysis CRS and geodesic) and the polygon’s area (flat and geodesic). Report units, method and rounding.
  5. Explain each flat/geodesic ratio by method (6.7).
  6. Hand-check one distance from the coordinates alone.
  7. Submit the log, outputs, untouched originals and the hand-check.

6.9.4Oral explanation (10 points)

In no more than three minutes, using request Q1 as the example, explain to a non-GIS colleague the difference between assigning a CRS and transforming data, what happens to the stored numbers in each case, and why a map that “looks right” is not proof that the right one was done.

Progression rule

Do not progress a learner who confuses assignment with transformation or degrees with metres, even if the final map is aligned.
