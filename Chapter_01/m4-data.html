Data and its meaning

One record holds three kinds of information. And not every record is the same kind of thing.

In this module you will

  • Pull one request apart into geometry, attributes, and time.
  • Tell an observed event from an asset and from a boundary — and know which to update.
  • Ask who collected the data, when, why, and what is missing.

1.4.1One request, three kinds of information

Pick a request. Its record splits into where, what, and when.

Try it

Three informal observations (Chapter 3 gives the formal structure):

  • Geometry here is a single position, so each request is drawn as a point. Roads and wards need more than one coordinate — that is where lines and polygons come in. For now “geometry” means “the part of the record that is space”.
  • Attributes are ordinary typed columns. Their meanings are not self-evident: “Reopened” only means something because a process defines it. When you inherit a dataset, first find the definition of every code.
  • Time is more than one field. P5 has a reported time, a closed time (now empty), and, buried in a free-text note, two more events. Free text is where meaning goes to hide; a GIS cannot filter on “reopened after second call” reliably.
Developer view

A request is like an event object with a typed location property. Good analogy — except the location property has its own validity rules (is it on the grid? right units? does “on the boundary” count?) that a normal property does not.

1.4.2Event, asset, boundary

The fixture holds three fundamentally different kinds of record. Treating them alike causes real damage. Ask of every record: is this a thing, a happening, or a rule?

Observed event

Request P5. Something that happened at a time and place, as reported.

The observation should not change. Its handling status changes.

Updating means: add status events; never overwrite the original report.

Asset

Drain DR-0042. A thing that exists and has a current state.

Yes, it changes — condition, last inspection, replacement.

Updating means: change current-state fields; keep inspection history separately.

Administrative boundary

Ward A. An area defined by an authority — a rule about space.

Rarely changes, and only by that authority.

Updating means: replace with the new edition; keep the old one with its dates.

Misconception

“When the crew fixes the drain, we update the request’s coordinates and status to Resolved and we’re done.” Overwriting the observation destroys the evidence that report and asset were 11 m apart, that the request was reopened, and when. Six months later nobody can answer “how often are drain reports mislocated?”
