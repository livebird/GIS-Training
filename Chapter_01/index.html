Start with a decision,
not a map.

This chapter teaches what a GIS (Geographic Information System) is, how to turn a business question into a question about where, and how to spot the limits of an answer. It is written for developers who have never used GIS.

No software is needed. Every map on these pages uses made-up practice data from the chapter document — a small imaginary town, not a real place. Your progress is saved in this browser only.

The scenarioA small town, two wards, one road, six requests

An imaginary city office (think of a Municipal Corporation or Nagar Palika) looks after assets (things it maintains: streetlights, drains, trees), keeps a list of roads, divides the city into wards (the same wards you vote in), and receives service requests — what most Indian cities call complaints or grievances from citizens (“streetlight not working”, “pothole”, “drain blocked”). Field teams visit each request and write an inspection (a note of what they found).

We use the word request throughout, but read it as “citizen complaint” if that is more familiar.

The question that runs through the whole course is:

“Which unresolved service requests should an inspection team investigate first?”

The training grid

  • Flat, two-dimensional, measured in metres.
  • Coordinates are written (x, y): x to the right, y upward.
  • Ward A and Ward B are each 1,000 m squares (1 km² each). They share the edge at x = 1000.
  • Road R1 runs from (0, 500) to (2000, 500) and stops there.
  • It has no real-world location. Think of it as graph paper.
  • Dates are written year-month-day: 2026-09-02 means 2 September 2026.

ModulesWork through them in order

Plain wordsA few words you will see everywhere

GISGeographic Information System — software plus data plus people, for working with information that has a location. Not just “a map”.
RecordOne row in a table: one request, one streetlight, one ward.
LayerOne kind of record drawn on the map — the Requests layer, the Roads layer. You can switch each on or off.
Crew / field teamThe workers who go out and fix things. Their base is called the depot.
PolicyHere it simply means the rules the office decides (“high priority first”) — not an insurance or government policy.
Practice dataThe made-up town used in every example. The chapter document calls it the “fixture”.

OutcomesWhat you will be able to do

LO1Explain what a GIS is and does beyond drawing a map.
LO2Turn a decision into clear “where” questions, with every vague word pinned down.
LO3Recognise the six kinds of “where” question; tell straight-line distance (as the crow flies) from travel distance by road.
LO4List the data a question needs, who made it, and what it leaves out.
LO5Use a prepared map (show/hide layers, pan, zoom, select) and explain why hiding is not deleting.
LO6Name a limitation of a spatial conclusion and offer an alternative explanation.
